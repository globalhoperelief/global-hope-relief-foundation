01 / WHO WE ARE

A foundation for possibility

Hope is more than a promise.
It is a responsibility.

Global Hope Relief Foundation is an early-stage humanitarian initiative being developed in Dhaka, Bangladesh, by founder Mr Nur Alam Sarkar.

Our vision is to grow into a responsible organisation that helps communities facing poverty, crisis and barriers to opportunity. We believe humanitarian support should protect dignity, listen to local needs and be delivered with transparency.

Our immediate priority is to build the resources, partnerships and institutional capacity needed to grow our humanitarian work responsibly.

A growing library of introductory resources on humanitarian principles and community wellbeing. These explain our areas of interest; they are not reports of our own field activity.

FOOD SECURITY

Understanding food insecurity

Food insecurity is not only a lack of food. Availability, affordability, access, nutrition, safety and stability all matter. Effective responses start by understanding the barriers households face.

What responsible support considers

Needs assessments, local food preferences, safe storage, market conditions, inclusion of people with disabilities, and coordination with existing providers all help reduce harm and duplication.

EDUCATION

Why learning continuity matters

Conflict, disasters, poverty, displacement and illness can interrupt education. Learning continuity helps protect children's development, safety and future choices.

What inclusive education support means

It can include accessible materials, safe learning environments, attention to girls and marginalised learners, support for teachers and links to formal education services.

HEALTH & WELLBEING

Essential health access

People may face barriers such as cost, distance, information gaps and shortages of qualified services. Humanitarian initiatives should complement — never replace — professional health systems.

Safe ways to contribute

Health information should be accurate and accessible. Medical care must be delivered by appropriately qualified providers, with privacy and informed consent protected.

DISASTER PREPAREDNESS

Preparedness saves time and reduces risk

Preparedness includes understanding hazards, sharing reliable warnings, planning safe evacuation and identifying support for people who may need extra assistance.

Community-centred preparedness

Plans work better when they are locally informed, inclusive, practical, regularly reviewed and connected to official emergency services.

HUMANITARIAN PRINCIPLES

Humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence

These widely recognised humanitarian principles help guide assistance towards people in need while protecting dignity and trust.

Why principles matter

Humanity means alleviating suffering. Impartiality means assistance based on need without discrimination. Neutrality avoids taking sides in hostilities. Independence protects humanitarian decision-making.

ACCOUNTABILITY

Listening to the people affected

Accountability means people understand what an organisation is doing, can ask questions, give feedback and raise concerns safely.

What good accountability includes

Clear information, accessible feedback channels, confidentiality, safe complaints handling, transparent decisions and action in response to concerns.

CHILD SAFEGUARDING

Protecting children from harm

Any organisation working with or around children must put their safety, dignity, privacy and best interests first.

Core safeguards to establish

Written safeguarding policies, appropriate screening and training, safe reporting channels, consent for images and stories, and a clear response process for concerns.

PARTNERSHIPS

Why local partnerships matter

Local organisations and community leaders often understand context, language, risks and priorities. Strong partnerships can make support more relevant and sustainable.

What a responsible partnership needs

Shared objectives, clear roles, due diligence, realistic budgets, safeguarding standards, transparent reporting and respect for local leadership.

The knowledge centre is educational and introductory. It is not medical, legal or emergency advice. In an emergency, follow instructions from official local authorities and qualified responders.

Clear answers

Questions you

How can I express interest in supporting the foundation?

Complete the Funding & Partnership Interest Form. Our team can then review your enquiry and contact you to discuss possible next steps.

Are you currently running humanitarian projects?

No. The areas described on this website are proposed future programme directions. We do not currently operate field projects or provide direct assistance.

Does submitting the form make a donation?

No. The form only records your interest and contact details. It does not collect money or commit you to making a contribution. Any proposed support will be discussed directly and handled through an appropriate process.

How can an organisation or philanthropist contact you?

Please use the partnership enquiry email. We welcome written questions about our vision, current status and the steps required to develop the initiative responsibly. An enquiry is not a request to transfer funds.

Where do you plan to work?

We are based in Dhaka, Bangladesh, and our vision is to support people wherever serious humanitarian need arises, in Bangladesh or internationally, subject to resources, coordination and applicable requirements.

How will you demonstrate impact?

When authorised programmes begin, we intend to publish verified activity updates, explain how resources are used and report results honestly. We do not currently claim beneficiary numbers or programme outcomes.

Can volunteers or organisations collaborate?

Written expressions of interest are welcome. Any future engagement will depend on organisational readiness, appropriate checks, clear roles and applicable approvals.
